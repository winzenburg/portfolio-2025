How we calculate this

Everything runs in your browser. Nothing you type here is sent anywhere.

  • Monthly mortgage payment (standard amortization): P = L × r / (1 − (1 + r)−n), where L = price − down payment, r = annual rate ÷ 12, n = term in years × 12. If the rate is 0, P = L ÷ n.
  • Loan balance after k payments: B = L(1 + r)k − P((1 + r)k − 1) / r.
  • Renting cost: sum of 12 × monthly rent for each year, with rent rising by the annual increase each year: Σ 12 × rent × (1 + g)y−1.
  • Home value in year y: price × (1 + appreciation)y. Property tax and maintenance are figured on the value at the start of each year.
  • Buying net cost = down payment + closing costs + all mortgage payments + property tax + insurance + HOA + maintenance − roommate income − (sale price − selling costs − remaining loan balance).
  • Opportunity cost (optional): (down payment + closing costs) × ((1 + return)y − 1), added to the buying cost.
  • Break-even year: the first year where buying net cost is less than or equal to total rent paid. If that never happens in your window, we say so.

Not included: mortgage interest or property tax deductions, capital gains taxes, PMI, utilities (assumed similar either way), furnishing, vacancy or roommate turnover, and changes in rates or insurance premiums. Opportunity cost is off unless you turn it on.
